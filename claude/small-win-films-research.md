## Is what we posted something people search for?

Partly. The category is searched, the titles are not.

- Film 1 "For 40 Days, Nobody Stopped | An Animated Short Film" and film 2 "I Tried 4 Things to Fix a Bad Day | An Animated Short Film" contain no phrase people type. "Bad day" searches are all songs (Daniel Powter, Alvin and the Chipmunks). "Never give up" searches are songs and speeches.
- Story films are found mostly through Home and Suggested, not search, so the title and thumbnail must win the click from someone who was not looking for it.

## What people do search (YouTube autocomplete, ranked)

- "relatable animation", "relatable animation shorts" (strongest of everything tested), "relatable animation stories"
- "motivational animation", "motivational animation story", "motivational animation shorts", "motivational cartoon clips"
- "animated short film" + sad / love story / funny / horror / award winning
- "short film about" + love, friendship, kindness, bravery, insecurity, lying, bullying, loss, growing up
- "life lessons" and "animated story" are dominated by other channels and kids' content.

## What wins on small channels (outlier search, under 100k subscribers, last 12 months)

Long form, "motivational animated short film":
- "I'll Start Tomorrow" (Ember & Shadow, 11.4k subs): 637k views in 31 days, 8.6x. Procrastination.
- "One Brave Trunk" (PolyFilms): 6.4M views, 67x. "DUCKS" (AJ Jefferies): 2.2M, 17x.
- Warning: a wave of DOPAMINE-style copycat channels launched in the last month (Last Monk Films, Turning Point Films, Still Animated "DAY ONE - Nobody Feels Ready", LAST FRAME "ONE MINUTE", Red Shadow lap). All have under 400 views. Same title pattern as ours: "WORD | A Motivational Animated Short Film". This is the template look to avoid.

Long form, "animated short film sad": single-word or simple titles by tiny channels broke out on craft and story: "LONE WOLF" (588 subs, 47k, 16x), "Moving Day" (462 subs, 168k, 11x), "The Girl and the Dragon" (2.2k subs, 113k, 15x).

Shorts, "relatable animation": tiny channels get 5x to 9x within a day with relatable comparisons and POVs: "Every couple ever: 'I'm cold.'", "Strict Love vs Gentle Love", "'I HATE YOU!!' mom vs 'I love you anyway' mom", "POV: Leaving The Window Open In Summer", "Why Your Phone ALWAYS Lands Face Down".

Trending in the last 14 days: kindness Shorts are hot ("One Small Gesture Can Change Someone's Day" 2.75M in 11 days, "POV: BE KIND FOR NO REASON" 3.3M in 14 days).

Kids' moral stories ("Bunny Helps Mr. Turtle", 1.3M) win big but are made-for-kids content with low ad rates. Not this channel's lane.

## Revamp (APPROVED by Kenny, 17 Nov 2026)

1. Position the channel as relatable everyday-life animation: long films for watch time, standalone relatable Shorts for discovery (not just trailers of the long film).
2. Titles: name a situation the viewer recognises, then a searched phrase. Avoid "ONE WORD | A Motivational Animated Short Film".
3. Pick each film's subject from searched themes via "research topic for Small Win Films" (procedure in workflow.md).
4. Add a weekly relatable Short series made from the same character style: "Every [person] ever", "X vs Y", "POV:".
5. Before every new video, run keyword research and the trending check, and write every upload pack SEO-first.
