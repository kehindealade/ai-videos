# Video generation workflow summary (Money Map and Small Win Films)

Full standing rules live in AGENTS.md, memory/preferences.md, claude/yt-stats/workflow.md, claude/analytics-lessons.md, claude/readability-and-retention-rules.md, claude/vertical-video-rules.md, claude/voice-rules.md and claude/upload-checklist.md.

1. Topic research and target region (per platform)
YouTube long, YouTube Shorts and Facebook Reels are planned separately because each platform works differently. For each topic, also pick the best target region and timezone for that content (US Eastern by default for high-CPM/US topics, or UK, Europe, Australia, SE Asia, or US-UK overlap when the topic fits better there) and tailor examples, spelling, currency and language settings to match. Before picking a topic, run keyword research and a trend check using free YouTube autocomplete, claude/yt-stats/yt.py (when signed in), web search for news, and vidIQ when it has credits. Target the keyword gap: high or medium demand with low or medium competition. Every topic must pass the policy and repetition gate: not already in the channel log, adults only (no kids), no Nigerian names, no politics, no named real people, no graphic content, and Money Map explains mechanics without giving financial advice. Give options with demand, competition, format and gate result.

2. Script and approval
Write the script with the hook type and opening line named at the top. Frame 1 must already move, have a clear sound, and pose a question or tension in 8 words or fewer. For Money Map, re-verify every number against real sources on build day and shape spoken lines with mood tags and natural pauses. For Small Win Films, send the full scene-by-scene script and wait for Kenny's go before drawing anything.

3. New design and engine setup
Read the channel log first (claude/money-map-style-log.md plus entries 12 to 15, or claude/film-log.md) so nothing repeats: new look, palette, structure, hook type, counter style, music, end card and cover. Copy the closest engine folder from ENGINE-INDEX.md into a new folder and redesign it. Everything is generated in code: Python and pycairo for frames, numpy for original music and sound effects, ffmpeg for encoding. No stock footage, no AI video models, no sampled music.

4. Voice and audio
Money Map and any spoken hook use Chatterbox Turbo by default, with a never-used Kokoro voice or two-voice blend as the reference clip (never a real person's voice). Each video, Short and Reel gets its own voice, with varied line speeds and gaps by mood, plus an optional quiet room-tone bed. Small Win Films films and Reels are music only (unless a Reel hook uses a voice). Mix and loudness-normalise to about -16 LUFS with true peak below -1 dBTP. Kenny listens once before posting since the AI cannot hear the output.

5. Stills and pre-render checks
Before rendering the full video, render key stills and a 15-second opening strip (one frame per second). Check that frame 1 matches the thumbnail, the first 30 seconds change visuals every 3 to 5 seconds for Money Map and every 2 to 3 seconds for story films, and everything reads at 390 px phone width. For 9:16 vertical videos, check that the picture fills all 1080x1920 pixels edge to edge (no borders, frames or letterbox bars so Meta does not flag it), and run a safe-zone overlay confirming all essential text sits inside y 300 to 1340 and x 90 to 990 (captions at y 1100 to 1300), at least 48 px, max 32 characters per line, 2 lines, contrast at least 4.5:1.

6. Rendering and platform cuts
Render the video (long videos in two parallel parts joined with ffmpeg concat), then mux the normalised audio. Every long video gets one YouTube Short, re-laid-out for vertical with its own design, voice and loop (never a plain crop). Facebook Reels are built to tell the whole story on their own, 35 to 60 seconds with the payoff before 0:45, never pointing to a YouTube long video.

7. Thumbnail and cover
Design a cliffhanger that raises a question without showing the answer or ending. For 16:9 thumbnails, follow left-to-right scanning (face or cause on the left, object or tension on the right), 2 to 5 words that add to the title instead of repeating it, and check with claude/yt-stats/thumbcheck.py so nothing sits in the duration badge or menu zones and everything reads at 320 px. For 9:16 covers, put the question block in y 384 to 900 and verify with the safe-zone overlay.

8. SEO, file naming and upload packs
Run autocomplete or yt.py seo for the main keyword. Name delivered files CHANNEL_platform_topic. Create one txt upload pack per video containing the title (under 60 characters, primary keyword in the first 40), unique description (with chapters and sources for Money Map, and no "animated with code" line), tags, hashtags, pinned comment question, SEO check, and a filled-in UPLOAD STEPS block. Include an .srt caption file for narrated videos.

9. Delivery, storage, API scheduling and logs
Only after passing claude/qa-gate.md (tools/qa.py + all 9 manual checks): upload rendered mp4, cover/thumbnail, upload pack and QA sheets into their own subfolder in Google Drive "AI videos/Renders/<Channel>/<Platform>/<topic>/" (e.g. Renders/Small Win Films/Facebook Reels/first-paycheck/ or Renders/Money Map/YouTube/social-security/), never loose in the Renders root. Check each channel and Page's last live post date first: if a channel or Page has gone several days without new content, priority is getting a new post out ASAP in the next best slot and target timezone to end the drought. Pick the earliest open best slot for the video's chosen target region/timezone (converted to Lagos WAT / UTC; no Facebook on Saturday; Shorts after their long video; never two posts on the same channel and platform in one day). Schedule directly from the VM using tools/yt_upload.py (YouTube video, thumbnail, metadata, .srt, publishAt) and tools/fb_reels.py (Facebook Reel, cover, caption, scheduled_publish_time, 10 min to 29 days ahead). Never commit media or secrets to GitHub. Update the channel log, voice-rules.md (if voiced), STATUS.md, the build plan, ENGINE-INDEX.md and memory notes, then commit and push. Set phone reminders and matching Google Calendar events in Africa/Lagos.

10. Post-upload check and analytics review
On YouTube, after tools/yt_upload.py uploads the video, open studio.youtube.com (Desktop site on Chrome Android or desktop) for a quick check: confirm green Copyright and Ad suitability icons, link the long video as Related video on Shorts, and confirm the scheduled time (until the OAuth app passes Google's free audit, API uploads land as Private and are flipped to Scheduled there). On Facebook, once tools/fb_reels.py schedules the Reel (or after it goes live), post and pin the first comment question (no engagement bait). About 48 hours after posting, run yt.py report plus check Studio and Facebook screenshots, verify whether viewer countries matched the target region, log retention, 3-second hold and search terms against the baseline in analytics-lessons.md, and apply lessons to future videos without re-editing old ones.

11. Model tiering and delegation (limit management)
Use lower/faster models (Flash, Flash-Lite, Haiku, Sonnet) for mechanical, routine and suitable tasks: rendering existing engines, audio muxing, running tools/qa.py, uploading and organizing Drive folders, API scheduling via yt_upload.py and fb_reels.py, writing upload packs, updating logs and simple code fixes. Reserve higher models (Pro, Opus) strictly for intensive creative or architectural work: writing new story film scripts, designing brand-new visual looks and pycairo scene engines from scratch, or complex multi-file debugging. When delegating to subagents, match the model tier to the task complexity.

Final rule: always ask Kenny before changing a finished video or moving a post off its slot.

QA gate (added 26 Nov 2026): before any upload or "done", follow claude/qa-gate.md: safe loudness mux, python3 tools/qa.py, all 9 manual checks answered from the phone-size sheets, fix and re-run until everything passes.
