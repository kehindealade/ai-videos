TITLE (56 characters)
How Social Security Works: The Formula Behind Your Check

DESCRIPTION
How Social Security works, from the 6.2% payroll tax to the three-slice formula that sets your check, plus claiming age, the 2027 COLA and Medicare Part B.

We build one Social Security check cell by cell, like a spreadsheet. First the money coming in: 6.2% from you and 6.2% from your employer, capped at $192,300 of pay in 2027, paid out to today's beneficiaries through two trust funds. Then your record: how Social Security work credits add up (one credit per $1,970 earned in 2027, 40 to qualify), and how your 35 highest years become your average indexed monthly earnings.

Then the part that surprises people: how Social Security benefits are calculated with three slices (90%, 32% and 15%), which is why some dollars you earn count six times more than others. After that, how full retirement age, claiming at 62 or waiting to 70 change the check, how the cost of living adjustment works (3.6% for 2027), and why the deposit is smaller than the check once the Medicare Part B premium ($209.40 in 2027) comes out.

The worker in this video is a labelled example (average indexed earnings of $5,000 a month). Your own record and estimates are in a free my Social Security account at ssa.gov. This video explains how the system works; it is not financial advice.
