# Analytics lessons (apply to every future video, every platform)

Source: first analytics review, 21 Nov 2026 (Kenny's YouTube Studio and Facebook screenshots plus yt-stats data for Small Win Films). Do not re-edit videos already made; apply these to every new long video, film, Short, Facebook Reel and TikTok on both channels. Add new lessons at the bottom with a date after each review.

## Baseline numbers (beat these)

- YouTube long film (Bad Day, 3:09): 46% still watching at 0:11, 25% at 0:40, 21% at 1:10, 18% finish. Average view 47 s (25%). YouTube rated the opening far below similar videos (relative retention 0.07 to 0.09), while the middle was much better (0.5).
- YouTube by device: phone average view 17 s, TV 65 s, desktop 95 s.
- YouTube Shorts: Bad Day Short about 100 views in its first 36 hours, more than both long films together. Money Map Bitcoin Short 23 views in its first day; bank Short 7 in 3 days.
- YouTube long videos: Two Cups 19, Bad Day 39, banks 10, Bitcoin 3 views. Long videos get almost no reach yet.
- Money Map traffic: 52.1% from YouTube search (the one source that works for it). Small Win Films: Shorts feed 44.6%, Suggested 39.5%, search 8.7% (only a false match).
- Facebook Reels: about 230 views each, then they flatten after about 6 hours. 3-second hold: Small Win Films 22% (51 of 229), Money Map 27% (73 of 271). 1-minute views: 2 of 229 and 6 of 271. Follows 0 on both Pages.
- Audience: 100% new viewers, 0 subscribers on Small Win Films; 34.9% of its views from the Philippines (Shorts feed).

## Rules from these numbers

1. Opening (all videos, all platforms): the first frame is the most dramatic or surprising frame of the video, with motion and sound on frame 1 and big on-screen text that asks a question or states the tension (8 words or fewer). No slow setup, no establishing shots, no title cards before the hook. Goal: more than 40% still watching at 3 s on Facebook, more than 60% at 0:10 on YouTube long videos.
2. Story films: the cold open is at most 5 seconds, then the story starts at once. Something new happens on screen every 2 to 3 seconds in the first 30 seconds. The question raised in the first seconds must be clearly in play by 0:30, since viewers who reach 1:00 stay to the end.
3. Phone first: most viewers are on phones and leave after 17 s on average. Text at least 2x the size that reads on desktop, faces large (at least a third of the frame height), one idea per screen, check every key frame at phone size before rendering.
4. Facebook Reels and TikTok: 45 to 60 seconds maximum (1-minute views are near zero). The payoff (twist, answer, ending) lands before 0:45. Still a complete story on its own, full-bleed 9:16 (see claude/vertical-video-rules.md).
5. Shorts are the reach engine: every long video or film gets at least 2 or 3 Shorts, each a complete mini-story with a loop and the long video set as the Related video. Plan the posting calendar so a Short goes out most days.
6. Money Map topics: choose evergreen, searchable topics ("how X makes money", "why is X so expensive") for long videos, since search drives the channel. News topics (crashes, price spikes) go out as Shorts and Reels only, unless the keyword-gap scan shows low competition for a long video.
7. Give people a reason to follow: recurring series names, a consistent character or format per series, an end card that names the next episode, a pinned comment question on YouTube, and a follow line on the Facebook end card. Track subscribers and follows per video.
8. Small Win Films search: titles and descriptions must target phrases people type for the story (from `suggest`), since search currently brings almost nothing. Check after 7 days which search terms found each film.
9. After every post, record in the channel log: Facebook 3-second rate and 1-minute views, YouTube retention at 0:10, 0:30 and the end, phone average view, traffic sources and search terms. Compare against the baseline above and note which change helped.
