## 4. Money Map, YouTube long video: Who Does America Owe $39 Trillion?

Title: Who Does America Owe $39 Trillion? (Not Who You Think)
Primary keyword: who does america owe money to. Secondary: who owns us debt, how much does america owe china, who does the us owe debt to.
Length: about 10 min (mid-roll ads need 8+). Voice: bm_lewis 0.94 (new to the channel). Category Education.
Look (new): "IOU wall". A dark green bank-vault wall covered in engraved paper IOU certificates (guilloche borders, serial numbers), each certificate labelled with who holds it; a brass ticker board on top showing the total. Fonts: a serif engraved style for certificates, condensed sans for labels.
Structure (new): myth vs reality. Open with the common belief, then five "holders" revealed one by one from smallest surprise to biggest, then a pie built from the certificates, then what happens when the debt is paid back.
Hook (unexpected outcome): "Most of America's debt isn't owed to China. It's owed to Americans."
Analytics lessons: frame 1 is the ticker reading $39 trillion with the China myth stamped FALSE (same as the thumbnail), a new picture every 3 to 5 s in the first 30 s, the hook question answered by 0:45 (the first big reveal), phone-size text, chapters, a reason to follow at the end (next episode named).
Verify on build day: total debt from Treasury "Debt to the Penny" (sources differ: $39.07T per govspending.org week of 11 Nov 2026; $40.58T per primerates.com on 10 Nov 2026). Title number follows the Treasury figure on the build day.

Chapters
0:00 Who does America owe?
0:45 The biggest lender is America itself
2:30 The Federal Reserve
4:00 Foreign countries: Japan, the UK and China
6:00 Everyone else: funds, banks and you
7:45 How the money gets paid back
9:15 So who does America owe?

Script

[0:00] Brass ticker flips to $39 TRILLION. A certificate labelled CHINA slides in; a red stamp hits it: FALSE.
"America owes about thirty-nine trillion dollars. Most people think China holds most of it. It doesn't. Most of America's debt is owed to Americans."

[0:10] The wall of certificates appears, blank.
"Every dollar of that debt is an IOU. The government borrowed money and wrote a promise to pay it back, with interest. Somebody holds every one of those promises. So who?"

[0:22] Five empty frames light up on the wall with question marks.
"There are five big groups. Let's open the vault."

[0:30] Camera pushes into the first frame.
"And the biggest one might surprise you."

[0:45] CHAPTER: The biggest lender is America itself.
Certificate stack labelled FEDERAL TRUST FUNDS grows to $7.63T, 19.5%.
"About a fifth of the debt, seven point six trillion dollars, is owed by the government to itself. Trust funds like Social Security collect more than they pay out in some years, and by law that extra money is lent to the Treasury. So part of what you paid into Social Security is sitting here, as an IOU."
Visual: a paycheck slip with "Social Security" deduction flows into the trust fund box, which turns into certificates.

[2:30] CHAPTER: The Federal Reserve.
Stack labelled FEDERAL RESERVE grows to $4.38T, 11.2%.
"The Federal Reserve holds about four point four trillion. The Fed buys government bonds from banks and investors as one of the ways it manages interest rates and the money supply. When it buys a bond, it holds the IOU, and the interest it earns mostly goes back to the Treasury after its costs."
Visual: a dial labelled INTEREST RATES; a hand moves certificates from a bank's shelf to the Fed's vault.

[4:00] CHAPTER: Foreign countries.
Stack labelled FOREIGN grows to $9.36T, 23.9%. Then three flags slide out of it as bars.
"Foreign governments and investors hold about nine point four trillion, roughly a quarter. And the biggest foreign lender isn't China."
Bars: JAPAN $1.07T, UNITED KINGDOM $1.01T, CHINA $0.59T (August 2026).
"Japan holds about one point one trillion. The United Kingdom about one trillion. China about five hundred and ninety billion, and China has been holding less year after year."
"Why do other countries buy American IOUs? Countries that sell a lot to America earn dollars. US government bonds are one of the safest places to park those dollars and earn interest."
Visual: a container ship unloads goods, dollars flow back, dollars turn into certificates in a vault marked TOKYO, LONDON, BEIJING.

[6:00] CHAPTER: Everyone else.
Biggest stack: ALL OTHER HOLDERS $17.70T, 45.3%.
"The biggest group of all, almost half, is everyone else: banks, mutual funds, pension funds, insurance companies, state governments, and people. If you have a retirement fund, a money market fund, or Treasury bonds, you may own a small piece of this."
Visual: the stack splits into small icons: bank, pension, fund, a person with a phone app.

[7:45] CHAPTER: How the money gets paid back.
A certificate with a due date ticks to zero; a new certificate is printed to replace it.
"When an IOU comes due, the Treasury pays it back, often by selling a new IOU to someone else. Every year it also pays interest to all these holders. That interest goes to trust funds, to the Fed, to foreign holders and to American savers."
Visual: interest coins flow out to the five groups in the same proportions.

[9:15] CHAPTER: So who does America owe?
The five stacks rearrange into a pie: trust funds 19.5%, Fed 11.2%, foreign 23.9%, everyone else 45.3%.
"So, who does America owe thirty-nine trillion dollars? About three quarters is held inside America: by government trust funds, the Federal Reserve, and American banks, funds and savers. About a quarter is held abroad, and Japan, not China, holds the most."
Final frame: the CHINA certificate from the opening, now small next to the giant American stacks.
"Next on Money Map: where the interest on this debt actually goes. Follow the money."

Sources (description): Treasury Debt to the Penny (total, build day); govspending.org "Who Owns the US Debt?" week of 11 Nov 2026 (trust funds $7.63T 19.5%, Fed $4.38T 11.2%, foreign $9.36T 23.9%, others $17.70T 45.3%); Treasury International Capital data via davemanuel.com, August 2026 (Japan $1,068.3B, UK $1,009.5B, China $590.7B).
Thumbnail: left, a tiny certificate with a Chinese flag and a red FALSE stamp; right, a huge stack of certificates with an American flag and "$39T"; text top "NOT CHINA". Cliffhanger: who is it, then?
Gate: borderline topic, kept neutral: explains who holds the debt from official data; no blame, no party, no policy opinion, no "the debt is good/bad" verdict.

Shorts from this video (2):
- Short A "Not China" (35 s, voice bm_daniel): hook "The biggest foreign lender to America isn't China." Bars race: Japan, UK, China. Ends: "So who's number one overall? It's America." Loops to the first bar.
- Short B "Your Social Security is an IOU" (35 s, voice am_liam): hook "Part of your paycheck is lent to the government." Paycheck slip to trust fund to certificate. Ends on the $7.63T stack.
