TITLE (61 characters)
How Credit Card Rewards Work: Who Really Pays for Your Points

DESCRIPTION
How credit card rewards work: one $100 purchase followed through five players, from the swipe fee to your 1.5% cash back, and who actually pays for it.

We follow $100 through an isometric city: you, the store, the store's bank (the acquirer), the card network and your own bank (the issuer). In about two seconds the card is approved, but nothing is paid yet. The real money moves in a batch a day or two later, with about $2.52 sliced off the sale: about $1.90 interchange to your bank, about $0.14 to the network and about $0.48 to the store's bank, so the store receives about $97.48.

Then the rewards: 1.5% back is $1.50, which is most of your bank's $1.90 slice, and the rest helps pay for fraud losses, running the card and profit. We explain why premium cards pay richer rewards (and charge $395 to $895 annual fees), how stores can pass card fees into every price (a July 2026 NBER working paper estimates about $30 billion a year moves from cash and debit shoppers to card users, with caveats), the three ways a card makes money (swipe fees, interest and annual fees), what one point can be worth, and a full year of $20,000 spending on a 1.5% card.

All figures are labelled typical examples, not a receipt. Real rates vary by card, store and country. This video explains how the system works; it is not financial advice and takes no side in the swipe-fee debate.
